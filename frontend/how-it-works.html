01 / 04

Upload and client-side privacy scrub

When an X-ray is dropped into Verifact, sensitive Protected Health Information (PHI) is scrubbed inside your browser's local memory before the pixel data ever reaches any inference model. Patient names, medical record numbers, and birth dates in DICOM Group 0010 tags are irreversibly purged. Additionally, margin borders are blanked out to catch burned-in radiographer annotations.

02 / 04

Two independent AI engines analyze the radiograph

Rather than relying on a single black-box algorithm, Verifact runs two complementary models in parallel. A lightweight local convolutional neural network (DenseNet-121) extracts dense feature maps to detect acute cortical bone disruption and builds an attention gradient (Grad-CAM heatmap). Concurrently, a clinical vision-language model evaluates anatomical alignment and drafts a 3-sentence radiological impression.

03 / 04

Unsure or disagreeing cases trigger priority triage

The system does not treat all predictions equally. If the two models disagree, or if the estimated probability falls into the algorithmic uncertainty band (between 40% and 70%), the study is elevated to the Urgent Discordance or Priority Review queue. Clear, unambiguous cases with high concordance are cataloged as routine, allowing radiologists to prioritize occult fractures and critical emergencies without delay.

Why this matters: False negative risk is drastically minimized by catching subtle hairline fractures in the uncertainty band before study sign-off.

04 / 04

A physician reviews, edits, and electronically signs off

Verifact is strictly decision support—it never finalizes a diagnosis or submits an EHR bill independently. The attending radiologist inspects the radiograph in the diagnostic workstation with calibrated millimeter calipers, adjusts the Grad-CAM blend, edits the AI-generated draft report directly in plain English, and applies a cryptographically sealed digital signature.
